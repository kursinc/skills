---
name: editorial-thought-leadership
description: Edit essays, articles, and executive thought leadership for argumentative clarity, structural coherence, logical precision, and a recognisable authorial voice. Use when a draft is repetitive, over-explained, example-heavy, rhetorically polished but logically weak, too tactical, or insufficiently tied to its governing commercial argument.
---

Edit from the largest unit of thought to the smallest:

**argument → architecture → selection → paragraph → sentence → voice → ending**

Do not polish a sentence whose paragraph or section has not yet earned its place.

The goal is a draft that sounds like the author on a very good day: clearer, tighter, more logically exact, and more confident, without replacing the author with a generic executive voice.

Read any supplied voice guide before editing. Treat it as binding editorial context.

The intellectual lineage behind the workflow is disclosed in [`LINEAGE.md`](LINEAGE.md). Use the methods below rather than imitating any source's prose style.

## 1. Establish the argument contract

Write four sentences privately before editing:

1. **Claim** — what the piece wants the reader to believe.
2. **Problem** — why the familiar view is inadequate or misleading.
3. **Grounds** — what reasoning or evidence makes the claim credible.
4. **Consequence** — what should change in leadership judgement.

Identify the governing tension and the commercial or practical standard against which the argument is judged.

Distinguish the main claim from observations that are merely true. A true point belongs only when it helps earn the claim.

**Complete when:** the draft has one claim whose reasons, evidence, qualifications, and consequences can be stated without referring to section titles.

## 2. Build the argument pyramid

Write each section's job in one short clause. Arrange those jobs as a hierarchy:

- the governing claim;
- the few major reasons supporting it;
- the evidence, examples, and implications beneath each reason.

Check that ideas grouped together answer the same question and sit at the same logical level. Move, merge, or remove sections that perform the same job.

Read the title and headings alone. They should reveal the progression of the argument without sounding like a consulting slide deck.

**Complete when:** every section supports the governing claim through a distinct reason, complication, or consequence, and no section survives on topical relevance alone.

## 3. Edit by selection

Treat selection as the editor's main instrument.

For every example, qualification, feature, consequence, and aside, ask:

> What changes in the reader's understanding if this remains?

Keep material that makes an abstraction concrete, introduces a genuinely different mechanism, establishes a necessary boundary, or moves the argument forward.

Assume one example can carry a section. A second earns its place only by changing the idea rather than changing the setting.

Compress catalogues into the distinction they collectively demonstrate. Retain only the details needed to make that distinction credible.

**Complete when:** every retained example and qualification changes the argument, and removing any remaining paragraph would create a specific intellectual loss.

## 4. Repair paragraph movement

Give each paragraph one centre of gravity.

The opening sentence should connect to what the reader already knows or to the question currently being answered. The paragraph should then develop new information towards its point of emphasis.

Check the movement between paragraphs:

- Does this paragraph answer the question raised by the previous one?
- Does its ending create the need for what follows?
- Has the conceptual subject remained visible, or disappeared beneath abstractions and nominalisations?

Join paragraphs that divide one thought artificially. Split paragraphs that carry unrelated argumentative work.

**Complete when:** the reader can follow the development of the idea without relying on headings, repeated summaries, or mechanical signposting.

## 5. Run the editorial pressure tests

Apply every relevant test in [`PRESSURE_TESTS.md`](PRESSURE_TESTS.md).

Pay particular attention to:

- examples doing the same intellectual work;
- lists of consequences standing in for a sharper distinction;
- contrast-by-negation becoming the essay's habitual movement;
- conclusions that recap the article as a checklist;
- memorable lines whose syntax conceals a category error or unsupported relationship;
- technology claims that outrun the organisation's ability to use the technology well;
- diagnoses that imply practitioner negligence where reasonable local decisions explain the outcome;
- local metrics presented without reference to the commercial result as a whole.

**Complete when:** every flagged passage has been cut, compressed, combined, or rewritten to advance a positive and logically supportable claim.

## 6. Test every sentence as thought

Read each sentence independently, then in sequence.

Ask:

- What exact claim does this sentence make?
- Can its nouns and verbs sustain that relationship literally?
- Does it follow from the sentence before it?
- Does its rhythm make it sound truer than the reasoning allows?
- Is contrast doing necessary intellectual work, or merely supplying shape?
- Are abstract words such as value, learning, quality, scale, harm, and transformation attached to concrete referents?

Prefer the precise relationship over the balanced phrase. Repair category errors, false parallels, ambiguous causality, and conclusions that outrun their premises.

**Complete when:** the strongest-sounding sentences can be paraphrased plainly without exposing a weaker argument.

## 7. Protect the author–editor covenant

Preserve the author's actual opinions, rhythm, humour, scepticism, and degree of certainty.

Edit the work hard while leaving the ownership of the thought with the author. Strengthen the structure around a distinctive phrase before replacing it. Keep expressive language when it performs intellectual work.

Prefer spoken, considered prose with varied sentence length. Let connected thoughts remain connected. Use contractions where natural. Remove institutional polish, synthetic balance, and phrases the author would never say.

Where a voice guide exists, check the final draft against it explicitly rather than relying on memory.

**Complete when:** the draft is more disciplined and recognisably the same writer, with no passage that sounds borrowed from an editor, consultancy, academic paper, or generic AI voice.

## 8. Preserve executive altitude

Keep public thought leadership centred on diagnosis, interpretation, and leadership judgement.

Expose the governing tension and the few distinctions needed to see it clearly. Remove workshop sequences, scoring systems, implementation checklists, tactical prompts, and exhaustive feature descriptions unless practical instruction is the stated purpose.

When discussing a platform or product, connect capabilities to the organisational problem they make more governable. The platform provides an operating environment; people supply commercial intent, evidence, authority, and judgement.

**Complete when:** the reader leaves with a better way to judge the problem, without receiving a substitute for the work required to solve it.

## 9. Edit the ending last

The ending should produce **residue**: the thought that remains after the examples and explanations fall away.

Return to the argument contract. Compress the claim, consequence, and unresolved governing tension into prose.

Use questions only when they leave the reader with a stronger act of judgement than a declarative ending would. Keep no more than two or three, and require each to carry a substantial part of the thesis.

Remove summary questions, section-by-section recaps, new examples, and late qualifications.

**Complete when:** the final paragraph could only belong to this essay and leaves the reader with its governing tension rather than an administrative checklist.

## 10. Read aloud and perform the final integrity pass

Read the complete piece aloud for cadence, repetition, and manufactured emphasis.

Then verify:

- every section has one distinct job;
- every example changes understanding;
- every major claim has adequate reasons or evidence;
- each paragraph moves from established context to new emphasis;
- contrast is occasional rather than structural;
- memorable language survives literal scrutiny;
- practitioner intent, leadership responsibility, and platform capability are represented proportionately;
- the conclusion contains the thesis without replaying the article;
- the author's voice remains recognisable.

**Complete when:** no local improvement weakens the whole argument, and no elegant line is carrying work the logic has not earned.

## Output

Return the edited piece, followed by a concise editorial note covering only material decisions about argument, structure, selection, logic, or voice. Do not narrate routine copy edits.

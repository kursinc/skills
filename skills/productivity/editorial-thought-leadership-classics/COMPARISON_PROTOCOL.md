# Comparison protocol

Use this protocol to compare `editorial-thought-leadership` with `editorial-thought-leadership-classics`.

The purpose is to identify a material improvement in editorial output, not to reward the skill with the more elaborate explanation.

## Prepare the test

Use the same unedited source draft, voice guide, audience, publication context, and requested length for both runs.

Run each skill in a fresh context when possible. Do not show either skill the other output or editorial memorandum.

Label the resulting drafts **A** and **B** without revealing which skill produced them.

Keep the word-count constraint identical. If one draft exceeds it, allow one compression pass under the same skill before comparison.

## Evaluate the drafts

Judge the finished pieces before reading the editorial memoranda.

For each dimension, choose:

- **A materially stronger**;
- **A slightly stronger**;
- **no meaningful difference**;
- **B slightly stronger**;
- **B materially stronger**.

Require a quoted passage or precise structural observation for every non-neutral judgement.

### 1. Governing argument

Can the reader state the central claim and why it matters?

Does the piece earn that claim through reasons and evidence rather than repetition or assertion?

### 2. Structural progression

Does each section perform distinct work?

Does the sequence develop the argument, or could sections be rearranged without intellectual loss?

### 3. Selection and proportion

Do examples, qualifications, and details earn their space?

Has either draft retained material because it is merely true, polished, or easy to illustrate?

### 4. Logical integrity

Do memorable sentences make literal sense?

Are causal relationships, parallels, categories, and commercial claims supportable?

### 5. Reader movement

Does each paragraph begin from a place the reader can follow and move towards a clear point of emphasis?

Are transitions earned by the developing thought rather than supplied by repeated signposting?

### 6. Rhetorical restraint

Are contrast, triads, questions, one-line emphasis, catalogues, and polished turns used deliberately?

Can the reader see the writing device more clearly than the idea?

### 7. Authorial voice

Which draft sounds more recognisably like the author?

Has either become smoother by losing conviction, rhythm, humour, cultural position, or natural speech?

### 8. Executive altitude

Does the piece improve leadership judgement without becoming a practitioner checklist or implementation guide?

Does it preserve enough reasoning for the reframe to feel earned?

### 9. Commercial and organisational precision

Are local outcomes connected to the commercial whole where relevant?

Are practitioner agency, leadership responsibility, and platform capability attributed proportionately?

### 10. Ending residue

Which ending leaves the more consequential thought?

Does it express the thesis and its consequence without replaying the article or assigning administrative homework?

## Decide the result

A challenger wins only when it is materially stronger on at least two dimensions and not materially weaker on voice or logical integrity.

A single exceptional sentence does not decide the test. Neither does word-count reduction by itself.

Record:

1. the winning output, or no meaningful winner;
2. the two largest differences;
3. one regression introduced by the winner;
4. the specific process change most likely to have caused each difference;
5. the rule worth transferring to the other skill.

## Test across multiple drafts

One essay reveals possibilities, not reliability.

Use at least three drafts with different dominant problems:

- a structurally loose draft with several valid but competing ideas;
- a polished draft whose rhetoric outruns its logic;
- a repetitive draft with strong voice and too many examples or qualifications.

Include a product-adjacent or platform thought-leadership piece when that is a primary use case.

Prefer the skill that produces stronger work across drafts, not the one best matched to a single failure mode.

## Compare the memoranda last

After judging the drafts, read the editorial memoranda.

A useful memorandum should explain the material decisions and identify genuine remaining vulnerabilities. It should not improve the perceived quality of a weaker edit through persuasive self-description.

## Completion gate

The comparison is complete when:

- both skills received equivalent inputs;
- draft identity remained hidden during evaluation;
- every material judgement cites evidence from the output;
- voice and logical integrity were treated as non-negotiable;
- the result identifies a transferable process improvement rather than merely naming a winner.

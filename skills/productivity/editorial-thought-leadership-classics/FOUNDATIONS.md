# Foundations

This skill synthesises methods from established books on nonfiction editing, argument, structure, clarity, and voice. It uses their durable editorial principles without reproducing protected text or imitating any author's style.

## Good Prose — Tracy Kidder and Richard Todd

**Contribution:** the author–editor covenant.

Editing is a relationship between rigorous judgement and respect for authorship. The editor helps the work become more fully itself rather than replacing the writer with an approved voice.

Applied here through:

- preserving ownership of the thought;
- editing structure before personality;
- distinguishing necessary intervention from stylistic colonisation;
- treating ethical and factual precision as part of prose quality.

## The Craft of Research — Wayne C. Booth, Gregory G. Colomb, Joseph M. Williams, Joseph Bizup, and William T. FitzGerald

**Contribution:** the architecture of argument.

A consequential argument requires a claim, reasons, evidence, warrants, qualifications, and a clear account of why the reader should care.

Applied here through:

- the reader–argument contract;
- explicit warrant testing;
- distinguishing evidence from illustration;
- separating truth-preserving qualification from defensive hedging;
- requiring the conclusion to express consequence.

## The Pyramid Principle — Barbara Minto

**Contribution:** logical hierarchy.

Complex ideas become intelligible when supporting thoughts are grouped by the question they answer, arranged at consistent logical levels, and subordinated to a governing point.

Applied here through:

- mapping parent and subordinate claims;
- giving each section one logical job;
- grouping like ideas;
- separating reasons, evidence, implications, and complications;
- using headings as a synopsis without importing presentation-deck prose.

## Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace — Joseph M. Williams and Joseph Bizup

**Contribution:** reader-centred movement.

Clear prose manages the reader's attention by moving from established context to new information, keeping conceptual subjects visible, and placing emphasis deliberately.

Applied here through:

- paragraph movement from known to new;
- question-led transitions;
- stable conceptual subjects;
- control of abstraction and nominalisation;
- emphasis that follows the argument rather than decoration.

## Several Short Sentences About Writing — Verlyn Klinkenborg

**Contribution:** sentence intention.

A sentence should be judged as an act of thought rather than accepted because paragraph momentum or rhythm makes it sound finished.

Applied here through:

- independent sentence testing;
- literal paraphrase;
- verb-relationship checks;
- detection of false parallels and category errors;
- resistance to automatic rhetorical shapes.

## Draft No. 4 — John McPhee

**Contribution:** selection and structure.

The writer and editor create form by choosing and arranging material. Accurate and interesting material still has to earn its place in the structure.

Applied here through:

- function-labelling examples and details;
- proportion across the whole piece;
- removal of material that is true but structurally idle;
- treating arrangement as part of meaning.

## On Writing Well — William Zinsser

**Contribution:** economy and human presence.

Clear nonfiction benefits from simplicity, confidence, disciplined removal of clutter, and a recognisable person behind the prose.

Applied here through:

- plain language;
- removal of institutional padding;
- economy without sterility;
- preference for personally credible expression over professionalised smoothness.

## They Say / I Say — Gerald Graff and Cathy Birkenstein

**Contribution:** recognition of argumentative moves.

Writers enter conversations through recurring moves such as framing an existing view, conceding, distinguishing, and responding. These moves are useful but become visible machinery when repeated.

Applied here diagnostically through:

- identifying stock contrast and concession patterns;
- retaining a move only while the reader needs it;
- replacing repeated templates with direct development of the positive claim.

## Limits

This is a synthesis, not a substitute for any book.

The skill does not:

- reproduce proprietary wording or extended passages;
- claim that the source authors would endorse this combined method;
- impose one source's prose style on the user;
- treat logical hierarchy as permission for consultancy tone;
- turn editorial judgement into a fully mechanical scoring exercise.

The sources provide lenses. The draft, audience, purpose, and author's voice determine the edit.

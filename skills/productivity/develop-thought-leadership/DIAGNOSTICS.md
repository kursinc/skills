# Development diagnostics

Use only the diagnostics triggered by the material. Record findings in the development brief rather than narrating the test process.

## Competing-essay test

For each plausible centre of gravity, ask:

- What reader judgement changes?
- Which material becomes essential?
- Which material becomes merely adjacent?
- Does the claim produce a consequential leadership or practical implication?
- Can the draft support it without importing a new essay's worth of evidence?

Prefer the candidate with the strongest combination of consequence, distinctiveness, support, and selective power.

## Claim–reason–evidence–warrant test

For every major claim:

1. State the reason it should be believed.
2. Name the evidence or example offered.
3. State the warrant connecting that evidence to the claim.
4. Identify the boundary or qualification.

Flag a claim when the evidence only illustrates possibility, the warrant is contestable but unstated, or the conclusion exceeds the available grounds.

## Category test

Check that compared terms belong to compatible logical categories. Common failures include:

- activity treated as capability;
- delivery evidence treated as commercial value;
- platform functionality treated as organisational judgement;
- a local metric treated as the combined business result;
- a symptom treated as the governing cause;
- an example treated as proof.

## Causal test

For every causal proposition, state:

- what changes;
- through what mechanism;
- under which conditions;
- what alternative explanation remains plausible;
- where the draft is making inference rather than reporting evidence.

Replace causal certainty with the strongest supportable relationship.

## Standard-consistency test

List every standard by which the essay judges success. Resolve contradictions in priority, time horizon, scope, or unit of analysis.

A useful standard should tell the reader how to judge trade-offs, not merely name several desirable outcomes.

## Agency test

For each outcome, build a row with actor, evidence, authority, incentives, cost/risk, and escalation route. Flag:

- blame assigned without control;
- leadership responsibility displaced onto practitioners;
- platform capability described as decision-making;
- costs carried by actors absent from the argument;
- passive constructions that conceal who chose or failed to choose.

## Product-role test

Write the platform's strategic role in one sentence. Test every product capability against it.

Retain a feature only when it makes the organisational problem more visible, governable, measurable, controlled, or executable. Cut feature inventories, generic benefit claims, and capabilities whose relevance depends on a different essay.

## Selection test

For each example, identify one function:

- make an abstraction visible;
- demonstrate a distinct mechanism;
- establish a boundary;
- supply evidence;
- reveal a consequence.

Keep the strongest example for each required function. Convert repeated examples into the governing distinction they share.

## Defensive-framing test

Mark qualifications, disclaimers, and anticipatory rebuttals. Keep those required by truth or reader trust. Replace editorial nervousness with a positive, bounded claim.

## Rhetorical-pressure test

Strip contrast, symmetry, triads, questions, metaphor, and one-line emphasis from the strongest formulations. Paraphrase them plainly.

Retain the device only when the literal proposition survives and the device increases precision or force rather than supplying the appearance of thought.

## Ending test

State the conclusion as a consequence of the governing claim. Check that it:

- follows from the established reasons;
- preserves the governing tension;
- contains no new evidence or administrative checklist;
- leaves a judgement that could belong only to this essay.
